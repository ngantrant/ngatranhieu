Manipal Institude of Technology

Project Name: Enhancing the Accuracy of Flux Estimation by Segmenting Lung Areas Using an Machine Learning-Based Approach from Free-Breathing, Non-Gated MRI Slices

  • Abtract: Patients with Thoracic Insufficiency Syndrome (TIS) often suffer from severe malformations of the chest wall, diaphragm, and spine, resulting in highly complex breathing mechanics. Traditional methods for assessing pulmonary function are inadequate for estimating lung capacity in these patients. A promising approach, termed ”Flux” was introduced to extract respiratory signals from non-gated, free breathing 3D MRI acquisitions of the thorax. Flux leverages optical flow, which captures the apparent motion of image features between consecutive frames, to estimate respiratory dynamics. This method involves segmenting the body region of interest and applying thresholds during Flux estimation to enhance accuracy. Flux-derived signals have shown potential for clinical diagnostics in both healthy subjects and TIS patients. However, in sagittal MRI scans, where the lungs and heart are simultaneously visualized, the interplay between cardiac and respiratory motion poses additional challenges for Flux estimation. The contraction and expansion of the heart during the cardiac cycle induce shifts in lung structures, complicating respiratory signal extraction. This study aims to enhance Flux estimation by integrating machine learning techniques, focusing on sagittal MRI data. Machine learning improved segmentation accuracy and better accommodate the dynamic interactions between the lungs and heart. The Flux estimation after segmentation reduces from the range [-1500;1500] to [-100:100] for the (z) location have the noise from heart and liver, the range [-1000;1000] to [-100;100] for the location affect by liver.  
  • Keyword: Thoracic Insufficiency Syndrome (TIS), Flux estiamtion, Machine Learning   
  • Publish Status: Under review
